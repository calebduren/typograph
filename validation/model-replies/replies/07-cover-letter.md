Here's a cover letter you can adapt for a product analyst role. I've kept it under 250 words, because most hiring managers won't read more than that.

---

Dear Dr. Okafor,

I'm writing to apply for the Product Analyst position at Lumen Health. Over the past four years I've helped a 60-person fintech team turn scattered spreadsheets into a single reporting layer that product managers actually use.

At my current company, I led the migration from ad hoc SQL to a governed metrics model. It cut the time to answer a standard retention question from two days to about 30 min, and it removed a recurring argument about whose numbers were right. I also built the experiment review process that now approves every A/B test before launch.

What draws me to Lumen is your focus on clinical outcomes rather than engagement. I've read the team's recent write-up on appointment no-shows, and I'd love to explore whether the same cohort approach could apply to follow-up adherence.

I'd welcome a conversation. I'm available most weekday afternoons after 2:00 p.m. and can be reached at alex.rivera@example.com.

Sincerely,
Alex Rivera

---

## Why this structure works

1. **Opening line:** the role and one credential. No "I am excited to apply."
2. **Middle paragraph:** one concrete result with a number. Numbers make claims believable.
3. **Third paragraph:** something specific about _them_. This is the part most applicants skip.
4. **Close:** a simple, low-pressure ask.

## Customizing it

- Replace the metrics with your own, and be honest about your role: "led" and "contributed to" are different claims.
- If you don't know the hiring manager's name, "Dear Hiring Team" is fine. Don't guess a title.
- Cut anything that also appears on your resume unless it needs context.
