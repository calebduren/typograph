# Setting up a standing desk for someone 5'11"

At 5'11" you're in a friendly range for most standard desks, but a few numbers matter more than the marketing suggests.

## Height

Stand with your elbows at 90 degrees and your forearms level. The desk surface should sit at that height. For most people who are 5'11", that lands between 42" and 45" from the floor. Many desks list a range of 25" to 50", which is fine, but check that the _low_ end works for sitting too. If you'll alternate, save both heights in the desk's memory presets.

## Monitor

A 24" monitor is a good match for a 30" deep desk. Sit an arm's length away, roughly 20" to 28" from your eyes. The top edge of the screen should be at or just below eye level, and if you wear progressive lenses you may want it a little lower.

| Setup         | Desk depth | Suggested monitor |
| ------------- | ---------- | ----------------- |
| Single screen | 24"-30"    | 24" to 27"        |
| Dual screen   | 30"-36"    | Two 24"           |
| Ultrawide     | 30"+       | 34"               |

## Cables

Standing desks move, so cables need slack. Measure the run from the wall outlet to the desk at its _highest_ position, then add a foot. I'd budget about 30' of cable in total for a home office with a monitor, laptop dock, lamp, and power strip. Mount the power strip to the underside of the desk and use a single cable to the wall so nothing gets pulled when the desk rises.

- Velcro straps beat zip ties, because you'll change things.
- A cable tray under the desk keeps slack off the floor.
- Label both ends of every cable. You'll thank yourself in a year.

## Anti-fatigue

Get a mat at least 3/4" thick, and don't stand for more than about 30 min at a stretch to start. Movement matters more than posture: shift your weight, step away, and sit when your lower back tells you to.
