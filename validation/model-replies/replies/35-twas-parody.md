Here's a light-hearted parody of the classic Christmas poem, set in an office on the last day before the holidays. It's meant for reading aloud.

---

'Twas the night before break, and all through the floor,
Not a laptop was open, not one at the door.
The badges were hung by the reader with care,
In hopes that no meetings would ever be there.

'Twas the last day of December, we'd all filed our reports,
And the coffee machine was in sorry old sorts.
Then out in the lobby there arose such a clatter,
I ran to the window to see what was the matter.

'Cause the CEO came with a tray in her hand,
And a smile that said "Yes, I have something planned."
"Now, Dana! Now, Marcus! Now, Priya and Sam!
On, interns! On, finance! Don't worry, I'm calm!"

---

## Notes on the parody

The first line of a stanza starting with an apostrophe is a feature, not a typo. The original poem opens with "'Twas," a contraction of "it was," and the apostrophe stands for the dropped letters.

## How I built it

1. **Kept the meter.** The original is anapestic tetrameter, so each line has four beats, with two unstressed syllables before each one.
2. **Kept the rhyme scheme.** Couplets throughout, which makes it easy to memorize.
3. **Swapped nouns.** "Stockings" became "badges," and "sugarplums" could become "calendar invites."
4. **Added a twist.** In the classic, the visitor is a surprise. Here, it's the executive who arrives with treats.
