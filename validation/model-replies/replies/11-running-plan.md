# A 5 km plan for someone who hasn't run in years

The goal isn't speed. It's getting to 5 km without being hurt or bored, and for most people that takes about eight weeks.

## Before you start

If you have a heart condition, are pregnant, or haven't exercised in a long time, check with your doctor first. As Dr. Lindqvist, a sports physician I'd trust, often says of new runners: too much, too soon is the only real injury plan. (That's a paraphrase; the advice is standard.)

## The structure

Run three days a week with at least one rest day between. Each session is a walk-run interval, and the run portion grows.

| Week | Session                                | Total time   |
| ---- | -------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| 1    | Run 1 min, walk 2 min, repeat 8 times  | 24 min       |
| 2    | Run 2 min, walk 2 min, repeat 6 times  | 24 min       |
| 3    | Run 3 min, walk 1 min, repeat 6 times  | 24 min       |
| 4    | Run 5 min, walk 1 min, repeat 4 times  | 24 min       |
| 5-6  | Run 8 min, walk 1 min, repeat 3 times  | 27 min       |
| 7    | Run 12 min, walk 1 min, repeat 2 times | 26 min       |
| 8    | Run 25-30 min continuously             | about 30 min |

By week eight you should be covering 5 km at a conversational pace, which for most beginners is 30 to 40 minutes.

## Effort

Use the talk test: you should be able to say a full sentence out loud. If you can't, slow down. Most beginners run their easy days too fast, and that's the most common reason plans fall apart.

## Shoes and surface

Get fitted at a running store rather than buying online. Softer surfaces like trails or a track are kinder to your joints than concrete, though that's less important than running consistently.

## Reading the body

- Sore muscles that ease as you warm up: normal.
- Sharp pain that gets worse as you run: stop.
- Shin pain on both sides: often too much volume, so repeat a week.

The figures in the research on injury rates (see Fig. 2 in most beginner-training guides) show that gradual increases matter more than any specific shoe.

## Staying on track

Put the sessions in your calendar like meetings. Tell a friend, or join a local group. Missing one session is fine; missing three in a row is when you should reset to the previous week.
