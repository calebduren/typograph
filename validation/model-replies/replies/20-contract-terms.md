# Reading the defined terms in a service agreement

Contracts use capitalized "defined terms" so they can refer to something long with a single word. Understanding how they work makes the whole document easier to read.

## How definitions appear

Usually there's a definitions section, or the term is defined at first use in parentheses:

> This Master Services Agreement (the "Agreement") is entered into by Acme Corp. ("Provider") and Beta LLC ("Customer").

From then on, "Agreement" means this specific document, "Provider" means Acme, and "Customer" means Beta. Any capitalized word should send you back to the definition.

## Terms to watch

- **"Services":** what exactly is being delivered. A vague definition can be the source of later disputes.
- **"Confidential Information":** check whether it's limited to items marked confidential or includes anything a reasonable person would understand to be.
- **"Affiliate":** a broad definition can extend obligations to a whole corporate family.
- **"Business Day":** matters for deadlines. Does it exclude holidays? In which country?
- **"Effective Date":** the day the clock starts, not necessarily the day it was signed.

## Common traps

1. **Circular definitions.** "Deliverables" means "the items described in the Statement of Work," and the Statement of Work says "Provider will deliver the Deliverables." Nothing is actually specified.
2. **Undefined capitalization.** If a capitalized term has no definition, ask what was meant.
3. **Inconsistent terms.** Using "Client" in one clause and "Customer" in another can open an argument about whether they're the same party.
4. **"Including" versus "including, without limitation."** Courts sometimes read the shorter version as a complete list.

## Questions to ask

- Who's bound by each obligation, and is that party defined precisely?
- Are the deadlines measured in calendar days or Business Days?
- What happens to "Confidential Information" after the term ends?
- Does the Agreement say which clause wins if two conflict?
