# Spanish for a week in Mexico City

You don't need much Spanish to be understood, but a small vocabulary helps a lot. Here's a survival set, grouped by situation.

## Greetings

- **Buenos días** (good morning) until about noon, then **buenas tardes**.
- **¿Cómo está?** How are you? (formal). Between friends: **¿Cómo estás?**
- **Mucho gusto.** Nice to meet you.
- **Con permiso.** Excuse me, when passing through a crowd.

## Ordering food

1. "Me da unos tacos al pastor, por favor." Give me some tacos al pastor, please.
2. "¿Qué me recomienda?" What do you recommend?
3. "La cuenta, por favor." The check, please.
4. "Sin picante, por favor." No spice, please.

Waiters won't rush you with the bill, and that's considered polite.

## Getting around

| Spanish                  | English                |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| ¿Dónde está el metro?    | Where's the metro?     |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta?          | How much does it cost? |
| Necesito un taxi.        | I need a taxi.         |
| A la izquierda / derecha | To the left / right    |

## Useful phrases that aren't in the textbooks

- **"¿Mande?"** A polite way of saying "pardon?" that you'll hear all the time.
- **"Ahorita"** means "right now," "in a little while," or "sometime," depending on context. Don't count on it.
- **"Está padre"** is slang for "it's great."

## Pronunciation

- **J** sounds like the English "h": "jalapeño" is "ha-la-PEH-nyo."
- **LL** sounds like "y."
- **H** is silent.
- Vowels are short and pure. Say "a" like the "a" in "father," and it's nearly always right.

## Attitude

Locals appreciate the attempt. Start with "Buenos días" and "por favor," and switch to English only when both of you are stuck. If you say "Perdón, mi español es muy básico," people are usually patient.
