# Why '90s alternative still sounds so alive

Ask people what they'd take to a desert island and a surprising number name something from the '90s. There are a few reasons that decade keeps its grip.

## The sound

Most '90s alternative records were made by bands who'd grown up on punk and college radio and who didn't much care about polish. Distorted guitars, loud-quiet-loud dynamics, and lyrics that were personal without being tidy. Producers of the era were often told to get out of the way, and the results sound like a room full of people playing at once.

## The business

Labels were flush with CD money and signed bands 'cause they'd sold well elsewhere, not because anyone understood them. That gave weird bands a shot they wouldn't get today. Give 'em a decent budget and a few months, and some of them made records that lasted.

## Where to start

If you want a short tour, I'd try these, in roughly this order:

1. **Nevermind**-era grunge, for the sheer force of it.
2. **Britpop**, for hooks and swagger.
3. **Lo-fi indie**, for songs that sound like they were written in a bedroom, because they were.
4. **Alt-country**, for the quieter side of the decade.

## Slang from the period

- "Sellout": someone who'd signed to a major and, supposedly, gave up their integrity.
- "Slacker": a label the media pinned on the whole generation.
- "'Til the wheels fall off": what fans told each other about the bands they loved.

## Listening tips

Don't stream everything on shuffle. These were albums, and the sequencing matters. Start at track one and stay 'til the end, then go back and pick the songs you'd have taped for a friend.

You might also try the live recordings. A lot of '90s bands were better on stage than in the studio, and the bootlegs that circulated on tape are now on streaming services.
