# A weeknight roast chicken that doesn't need babysitting

This is the recipe I'd hand to someone who says "I can't cook a whole bird." It's forgiving, and the technique is mostly waiting.

## Ingredients

- 1 whole chicken, about 4 lb
- 2 tablespoons butter, softened
- 1 lemon, halved
- 4 cloves garlic, smashed
- Salt and black pepper
- Thyme or rosemary, if you have it
- 1 onion, cut into 1" wedges

## Steps

1. **Dry the bird.** Take it out of the fridge an hour ahead and pat it very dry with paper towels. Dry skin is the secret to crispness.
2. **Season generously.** Salt inside and out. It looks like too much; it isn't. If you have time, salt it the night before and leave it uncovered in the fridge.
3. **Heat the oven** to 425 F with a rack in the lower third.
4. **Stuff and rub.** Put the lemon halves and garlic in the cavity. Rub the butter under and over the skin.
5. **Roast.** Scatter the onion wedges in a pan, set the chicken on top, and roast for 55 to 70 min. It's done when the thickest part of the thigh reads 165 F.
6. **Rest it** for 15 min before carving. Don't skip this; it's what keeps the juices in the meat.

## Cutting up the onions

Don't fuss over exact sizes. If the wedges are around 1" thick, they'll soften and caramelize in the drippings. Thinner slices burn.

## The pan sauce

Pour off most of the fat, leaving about a tablespoon. Put the pan over medium heat, add a splash of white wine or stock, and scrape up the browned bits. Simmer 'til it thickens slightly, then swirl in a knob of butter.

## Troubleshooting

- **Skin's not crisp:** the bird was wet, or the oven was too cool. Next time, dry longer and raise the heat.
- **Breast is dry:** you cooked to a higher number than needed. Pull it at 160 F and let carryover do the rest.
- **Pale color:** roast the last 10 min under the broiler.

## Leftovers

Pull all the meat off the bones while it's warm, and make stock with the carcass. It's the best thing you'll do with 20 min of effort, and you'll have soup that tastes like you worked at it.
