# Feedback on your poem

Thanks for sharing it. There's a real image at the center, the "kettle that argues with the morning," and I'd build the poem around it. Here are my notes, roughly in order of importance.

## What's working

- The opening line is concrete and surprising, which earns the reader's trust.
- The turn in stanza three, where the speaker admits she's "still listening," changes the poem from description to feeling.
- You resist a tidy ending, which is brave.

## What I'd change

1. "It was as if the world was holding its breath." This is the kind of phrase readers have met a thousand times. Replace it with something only your speaker would notice, like the sound the refrigerator makes when it stops.
2. "My heart is a lonely house." A metaphor that announces itself is weaker than one that's implied. Cut the sentence and keep the details of the house.
3. "Nothing is ever really gone." Endings that state the theme tend to flatten it. Let the last image carry the point.
4. "Silence" appears four times. Keep the strongest one.

## Line-level notes

The second stanza breaks after "the," which forces a pause on a weak word. Try ending the line on a noun or a verb, since line ends carry weight in free verse.

> the kettle begins its argument
> with the morning, and loses

I'd move "and loses" to its own line. The short line gives you a small comic beat, and it lets the tone shift before the quiet ending.

## A quick exercise

Read the poem aloud and mark every place you take a breath that the punctuation didn't ask for. Those are candidates for line breaks. Then read it again and mark every place you speed up. Those often signal words that aren't earning their place.

## On rhyme

You use a near rhyme in the second and fourth lines ("door" and "before") and drop it in stanza three. That inconsistency reads as accidental. Either keep the pattern or abandon it clearly.
