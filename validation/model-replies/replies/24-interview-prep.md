# Preparing for a behavioral interview

Behavioral questions sound open-ended, but they follow patterns. If you prepare six good stories, you can adapt them to nearly anything.

## The questions you'll hear

- "Tell me about yourself."
- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a colleague."
- "What's your biggest weakness?"
- "Describe a project that didn't go as planned."

## A structure for stories

Use the STAR format:

1. **Situation.** One or two sentences of context.
2. **Task.** What you were responsible for.
3. **Action.** What _you_ did, using "I," not "we."
4. **Result.** The outcome, with a number if you have one, plus what you learned.

Keep each story to about two minutes.

## Handling "Tell me about yourself"

This isn't an invitation to recite your resume. Give a three-part answer: where you are now, how you got here, and why you're interested in this role. Aim for 60 to 90 seconds.

## The weakness question

Pick a real weakness, not a disguised strength. "I work too hard" reads as evasion. A better answer: "I used to avoid asking for help, which slowed me down. Now I set a rule: if I'm stuck for 30 min, I ask."

## Stories to prepare

| Theme      | Prompt it covers                      |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Conflict   | Disagreements, difficult stakeholders |
| Failure    | Mistakes, missed deadlines            |
| Leadership | Influencing without authority         |
| Ambiguity  | Unclear requirements                  |
| Impact     | Your proudest result                  |
| Learning   | Picking up a new skill fast           |

## Questions to ask them

Always have questions. "What does success look like in the first 90 days?" and "What's the hardest part of this role that isn't in the description?" both work well.

## Day-of tips

- Arrive 10 minutes early, or log in 5 minutes early for video.
- Have a glass of water and a notepad within reach.
- It's fine to pause before answering. "Let me think about that for a moment" sounds confident.

## Practice

Record yourself answering three questions, then listen back. You'll hear filler words and rambling that you can't notice in the moment.
