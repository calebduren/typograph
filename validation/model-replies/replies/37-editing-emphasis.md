# Editing checklist: emphasis and quotation marks

Emphasis and quotes are two tools writers reach for when a sentence isn't working. Here's how to use each without cluttering the page.

## Italics, bold, and quotes do different jobs

- _Italics_ stress a word or mark a title.
- **Bold** helps readers scan, and it's best kept for headings or key terms.
- "Quotation marks" show that words come from someone else, or that you're mentioning a word rather than using it.

If you mix them, keep it consistent. A word that's both a term and a quote can be styled as _"cache"_, or as "_cache_" if the emphasis is inside the quote. Pick one and stick with it. The distinction is small, but readers notice inconsistency.

## Scare quotes

Putting a word in quotation marks to signal doubt is the writing equivalent of a wink. It can undermine your own claim: saying the "benefits" of a policy suggests there aren't any. If you mean it, say it plainly. If you don't, cut the marks.

## Where to put punctuation

In American English, periods and commas go inside the closing quote, even if they aren't part of the quotation: She called the plan "reasonable." Colons and semicolons go outside. Question marks depend on the sentence: Did she say "reasonable"? but She asked, "Is it reasonable?"

British style tends to place them logically, so check which convention your publisher or client uses.

## Emphasis in dialogue

Use italics for stress, but rarely. "I _told_ you not to touch that" works once per scene. If every character's line has an italic word, none of them mean anything.

## A quick pass

1. Search for every quotation mark and ask if it's needed.
2. Look for words in italics and bold, then remove half of them.
3. Read the sentence aloud. If you'd naturally stress a word, you may not need to style it.
4. Check that titles are italicized or quoted the same way every time.

## Before and after

Before: The "results" were _very_ **important**.

After: The results were important.

That last version says everything the first one did, without the shouting.

## When to keep them

Keep emphasis where the meaning changes with the stress, as in "I didn't say _she_ stole it." Keep quotes around actual quotations and words used as words. Everything else is a candidate for the cutting room.
