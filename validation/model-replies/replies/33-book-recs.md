# Books for someone new to science fiction

The best entry point depends on what you already enjoy, so here are picks grouped by taste. I've tried to include both classics and recent books.

## If you like character-driven stories

1. **"The Left Hand of Darkness"** by Ursula K. Le Guin. An envoy on a winter planet learns about a society without fixed gender. It's slow, thoughtful, and one of the most respected books in the field.
2. **"Kindred"** by Octavia Butler. A modern woman is pulled back to a plantation in the antebellum South. It's technically time travel, but it reads like a novel of survival.
3. **"Station Eleven"** by Emily St. John Mandel. A post-pandemic traveling theater troupe. It's quiet and literary.

## If you like big ideas

- **"Ender's Game"** by Orson Scott Card is fast and accessible, though its author is controversial and you may want to read reviews first.
- **"The Three-Body Problem"** by Liu Cixin is hard SF built on physics puzzles, and it grows enormous.
- **"Children of Time"** by Adrian Tchaikovsky imagines the evolution of an intelligent spider civilization, and it's more moving than that sounds.

## If you like humor

**"The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy"** by Douglas Adams remains the funniest introduction. Start there if you're not sure that SF is for you.

## If you like adventure

Try **"Project Hail Mary"** by Andy Weir, which mixes problem-solving with an unlikely friendship, or **"The Martian"**, also by Weir.

## How to choose

| If you liked...  | Try                         |
| ---------------- | --------------------------- |
| Literary fiction | "Station Eleven"            |
| Thrillers        | "Project Hail Mary"         |
| Comedy           | "The Hitchhiker's Guide"    |
| Essays and ideas | "The Left Hand of Darkness" |

## Short stories

If novels feel like a commitment, a story collection is a good way to sample. "Stories of Your Life and Others" by Ted Chiang is excellent, and the title story became the film _Arrival_.

## A few tips

- Don't finish a book you're not enjoying. Life's too short, and the field is big.
- Ask your library for staff picks.
- Audiobooks are a fine way in, especially for long books.
