# Useful French for a first trip to Paris

You don't need fluency, but a few phrases change how you're received. Start every interaction with "Bonjour" and you're already ahead.

## Greetings and manners

- **Bonjour, madame / monsieur.** Say it when you walk into any shop.
- **S'il vous plaît.** Please.
- **Merci beaucoup.** Thank you very much.
- **Excusez-moi.** Excuse me, for getting attention or squeezing past someone.
- **Je ne parle pas bien français.** I don't speak French well. Say it with a smile, then try anyway.

## In a restaurant

1. "Une table pour deux, s'il vous plaît." A table for two, please.
2. "L'addition, s'il vous plaît." The bill, please. Waiters won't bring it until you ask, and that's courtesy, not bad service.
3. "Qu'est-ce que vous recommandez?" What do you recommend?

## Names you'll meet

French names often contain apostrophes that are part of the spelling, not punctuation to be "fixed." You'll see L'Oréal in the pharmacy, d'Artagnan on a plaque near the Left Bank, and Jean d'Ormesson in the bookshop windows. English speakers tend to drop the apostrophe and the second half of the name, but locals notice when you get it right.

## Two pronunciation tips

- The final consonant is usually silent, so "petit" sounds like "puh-tee."
- The "r" comes from the back of the throat, but don't obsess over it. Clear, slow speech matters more.

## When someone answers in English

It isn't a rebuke. Many Parisians switch to English to be helpful or because it's faster. You can say "Je voudrais pratiquer mon français, si vous permettez" and most people will happily oblige. That means "I'd like to practice my French, if you don't mind."
