# Building simple garage shelves from lumber

You can make sturdy shelves for well under $150 with basic tools. This design uses standard lumber sizes, so there's very little cutting.

## What you'll need

- Four 2x4 boards, 8' long, for the uprights
- Six 2x4 boards, 8' long, for the cross braces
- Two sheets of 3/4" plywood, 4' x 8'
- 3" construction screws and 1 1/4" wood screws
- A drill, a saw, a level, and a tape measure

A note on naming: a "2x4" isn't 2" by 4". It's actually 1 1/2" by 3 1/2" after milling. This trips up everyone the first time.

## Plan the size

The finished unit is 8' wide, 24" deep, and 6' tall. If you're 5'11" or taller, you may want the top shelf at 6' 6" so you can see what's on it, but taller shelves need to be anchored to the wall.

## Steps

1. **Cut the plywood.** Rip each sheet into two 24" x 96" boards. Most home centers will cut it for you at the store.
2. **Build the end frames.** Each is two 2x4 uprights at 72" joined by cross braces at 12", 36", and 60". Use two 3" screws at each joint and pre-drill to avoid splitting.
3. **Stand the frames** about 8' apart, and connect them with a back brace near the top and bottom.
4. **Check square.** Measure both diagonals. If they match, the frame is square.
5. **Set the shelves.** Rest the plywood on the cross braces and screw it down with the smaller screws every 12".
6. **Anchor to the wall.** Screw the top brace into a stud with a lag screw. It matters more than it seems.

## Weight

Plywood shelves at this span will hold about 100 lb each if supported every 24". For heavier loads, add a center support. Don't stack paint cans on one edge, because uneven loads bend the plywood over time.

## Finishing

Sand the edges and apply a coat of polyurethane if the shelves will see moisture. A damp garage will swell raw plywood in a season or two.
