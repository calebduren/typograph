# Making dialogue sound like people

Good dialogue isn't a transcript. It's speech with the boring parts removed, so the trick is to keep what carries character or conflict and cut the rest.

## Punctuation basics

Put the speaker's words in double quotes and keep the attribution simple. "I told you I'd be here," Maria said, "and I'm here." A reader barely notices "said," which is exactly why it's the best tag you have.

When a character quotes someone else, use single quotes inside the double ones:

"My mother always said, 'Never trust a man who won't eat dessert,' and she was right about Tom."

"Right about him how?" Dana asked.

"He's on a diet. Enough said."

## Multi-paragraph speeches

If one person speaks across paragraphs, open each paragraph with a quote mark but only close the last one. That tells the reader the speech isn't finished yet:

"When I was young, my grandfather ran the ferry. He knew every current, every sandbar, and he never once used a chart.

"People asked how he did it. He'd shrug and say the river told him. I didn't believe that until I'd worked the boat myself for ten years."

## Techniques that help

1. **Interrupt.** Real people cut each other off. Use an em dash or trailing action instead of a tidy full sentence.
2. **Let subtext do the work.** A character who says "I'm fine" while slamming a cupboard is telling the reader two things.
3. **Use beats, not adverbs.** Instead of `"Leave," he said angrily`, try "Leave." He didn't look up from the letter.
4. **Read it aloud.** If you stumble, your reader will too.

> Dialogue is where the reader decides whether they believe your characters.

## Common mistakes

- Everyone speaks in complete, grammatical sentences.
- Characters explain things they both already know ("As you know, Bob, we've been partners for years").
- Every line has a tag, so the rhythm feels mechanical.
- Accents are spelled out phonetically until the page is unreadable.
